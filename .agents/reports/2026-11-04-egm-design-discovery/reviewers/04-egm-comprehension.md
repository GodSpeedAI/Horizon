## (C) Five ambiguities that would cause divergent implementation

1. **"Only process speaking to authorities."** Reading A: an OS-process invariant — product fronts never open SFWP sockets; operator resolve and approval list flow through the embedded Cognate runtime as governed capabilities. Reading B: a single-code-path convention — fronts import the SDK-exported `seaForgeCall` but make the calls themselves (which is literally what M-1 proposes). A builds a capability/gateway layer and INV-2 teeth that fail on any direct socket; B writes an adapter-import refactor and teeth that only check import provenance. T-10's raw-socket calls are violations under A and possibly compliant under B.

2. **Settlement.** Reading A: one mechanism — SEA-Forge's declaration ledger, human-gated by C-1, so C-2's "IS7 settlement" is just Horizon's *display* of a SEA-Forge declaration. Reading B: two mechanisms — SEA-Forge declares operational obligations (C-1), and Horizon separately performs an IS7 navigation judgment comparing expected outcomes against RealityTrace's three-branch evidence (C-2), which can disagree with the declaration. The two readings produce different owners for "is this settled," different failure states, and different J02r2 test assertions.

3. **Capacity.** Reading A: a durable Horizon-side ledger of funds with reserve → hold (`awaiting_authority`) → capture/release lifecycle, hence "stuck holds," "reclaim," and single-commit atomicity matter. Reading B: no ledger — affordability is a per-operation `payment` gate verdict computed at frame assessment, and M-4's "accounting" is just making two SQLite writes transactional. The rejection of the Cognate move is sound under A and possibly moot under B.

4. **M-3's re-bind.** Reading A: fold P1–P8 into the *existing* DAG as amendments (T11a/T23+), keeping spec 1.2.0 and the current plan artifact. Reading B: re-freeze a *new* plan whose tasks are the production-readiness classes. The readings fork the ledger, prereg directories, gate names, and evidence tree — precisely the dual-ledger defect (T-1) M-3 exists to cure.

5. **Approval resolve ownership and identity.** Reading A: resolve is a Cognate governed capability (`governance.approval.resolve`), with operator identity verified inside the runtime using `horizon/shared-identity`. Reading B: the product front calls SEA-Forge resolve directly with the SDK's typed client, asserting identity from configuration — M-2's `decision_id`/`criteria_sha256` guards staleness but nobody verifies *who* resolves, leaving 02 T-15 (identity "asserted by configuration, not verified at the boundary") half-open. The two readings place WebAuthn checks, service-vs-operator account separation, and the "already exists" service-actor-refusal test in different components.
